# Heatwave and Cold Wave (India)

Heat kills more Indians than any other weather hazard. Heatwaves in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Vidarbha, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, and Delhi routinely exceed 45 C, with wet-bulb conditions in coastal and eastern India that are physiologically more dangerous still. Cold waves in north India — Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar — kill hundreds of homeless and rural people every winter, and the Himalayan states face genuine hypothermia risk year-round at altitude.

---

## Part 1: Heat — Understanding the Danger

**IMD heatwave criteria:** a heatwave is declared when the maximum temperature reaches at least 40 C in the plains, 37 C on the coast, or 30 C in the hills, **and** is 4.5-6.4 C above normal. A **severe heatwave** is a departure of over 6.4 C, or an absolute maximum of 45 C (47 C for severe).

**Humidity matters more than temperature.** A dry 45 C in Jaisalmer is survivable in shade with water. A humid 38 C in Kolkata or Chennai can be more lethal, because sweat cannot evaporate and the body loses its only cooling mechanism. Watch the "feels like" / heat index, not just the thermometer.

**Highest risk:**
- Outdoor workers — construction, agriculture, delivery riders, street vendors, traffic police
- Infants and children under 5
- Adults over 65
- Pregnant women
- People with heart disease, kidney disease, diabetes, or obesity
- People on diuretics, antihistamines, antipsychotics, or beta-blockers
- People living in tin-roofed or asbestos-roofed houses, and in top-floor rooms
- Anyone without access to shade, water, or a fan during a power cut

## Part 2: The Three Heat Illnesses

**Heat cramps** — painful muscle spasms, usually in the legs or abdomen, after exertion and sweating.
- Stop, move to shade, rest
- Give ORS or salted water or nimbu-pani with salt
- Do not resume heavy work for several hours

**Heat exhaustion** — the body is losing the fight but is still compensating.
- **Signs:** heavy sweating, cool and clammy pale skin, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, fast weak pulse, muscle cramps, thirst, irritability, fainting
- **Treatment:** move to shade or an air-conditioned space, lie down, raise the legs, remove excess clothing, cool the skin with wet cloth and fanning, give ORS or cool water in small frequent sips
- **This is the warning stage. Act now and it does not become heat stroke.**

**Heat stroke — a medical emergency. This kills.**
- **Signs:** body temperature above 40 C; **confusion, agitation, slurred speech, seizures, or unconsciousness**; skin that is hot and often **dry** because sweating has stopped (though in exertional heat stroke the skin may still be wet); rapid breathing; a racing pulse; flushed skin
- **The distinguishing sign is a change in mental state.** A hot person who is confused, aggressive, or drowsy has heat stroke until proven otherwise.
- **Treatment — cool them aggressively and immediately. Cooling is the treatment; nothing else matters as much.**
  1. Move to shade at once
  2. **Remove all outer clothing**
  3. **Pour water over them continuously and fan hard.** Evaporative cooling in Indian conditions is highly effective and requires only water and a piece of cardboard.
  4. **Put ice packs, cold water bottles, or wet cloth in the armpits, groin, neck, and behind the knees** — where large blood vessels run near the surface
  5. **If a tub, tank, or drum is available, immerse them in cold water up to the neck** — this is the fastest cooling method and it saves lives. Hold the head above water.
  6. **Do not give fluids to an unconscious person.**
  7. **Do not give paracetamol or aspirin.** They do not work for heat stroke and aspirin worsens bleeding risk.
  8. **Do not use alcohol rubs.**
  9. **Get to a hospital**, but cool them first and continue cooling on the way. The first 30 minutes decide the outcome.

## Part 3: Surviving a Heatwave

**Water**
- Drink before you are thirsty. Thirst is a late signal in the heat.
- **Check your urine.** Pale straw yellow is fine. Dark yellow or amber means you are already dehydrated. Not passing urine for 8 hours is an emergency sign.
- Water alone is not enough during heavy sweating — you lose salt too. **ORS, nimbu-pani with salt and sugar, chaas/buttermilk, aam panna, coconut water, and sattu** all replace both.
- **Avoid alcohol, strong tea and coffee in excess, and sugary carbonated drinks** — all worsen dehydration.
- If you have kidney or heart disease and have been told to limit fluids, ask your doctor before increasing intake.

**Behaviour**
- **Stay indoors between 12 noon and 4 pm.** Reschedule all outdoor work to before 11 am and after 5 pm.
- If you must be outside: rest in shade for 10 minutes every hour, wear a wet cloth or gamcha on your head and neck, and drink continuously.
- **Never leave a child, an elderly person, or an animal in a parked vehicle.** The interior reaches 60 C+ in minutes even with windows cracked. This kills every summer.
- **Wear loose, light-coloured, cotton clothing.** Cover your head outdoors — a cap, a gamcha, a dupatta, or an umbrella. A wet cloth on the head is genuinely effective.

**Keeping a house cool without power**
- **Close windows, curtains, and shutters during the day** on the sun-facing side; open everything at night for cross ventilation
- **Wet a bedsheet or a khus/vetiver mat and hang it in the window.** Air passing through evaporates and cools — this is a genuinely effective traditional Indian technique
- **Wet the floor and the terrace** in the evening
- **Cool the roof.** A white lime wash, white paint, or a layer of straw, coconut leaves, or thermocol on a tin or concrete roof drops the indoor temperature by several degrees. This is the single highest-value intervention for tin-roofed homes.
- **Sleep on the floor or the terrace at night** — hot air rises
- **Wet a cloth and place it on the neck, wrists, and behind the knees**
- **Take cool showers**, or wet the arms and legs frequently
- **Turn off unnecessary electrical appliances** — they add heat

**Watch other people**
- **Check on elderly neighbours living alone at least twice a day.** Isolated elderly people are the largest heatwave death group and they will not ask for help.
- Watch infants for fewer wet nappies, sunken eyes or fontanelle, no tears when crying, and lethargy. Give ORS and seek help.
- Provide water for street animals and birds.

**Heat Action Plans:** Ahmedabad pioneered India's first city Heat Action Plan and dozens of cities now have them. They include cooling centres, extended hospital hours, water points, and rescheduled work timings. **Find out whether your city has one and where the cooling centres are.**

## Part 4: Cold Wave and Hypothermia

**IMD cold wave criteria:** minimum temperature at or below 10 C in the plains with a departure of 4.5 C or more from normal, or a minimum at or below 4 C regardless.

**Cold kills in India at temperatures people dismiss as mild.** Most Indian cold deaths occur between 5 and 12 C, in people who are wet, undernourished, exposed, or alcohol-affected. Wet clothing at 10 C is more dangerous than dry clothing at 0 C.

**Hypothermia stages:**
- **Mild:** shivering, cold hands and feet, tiredness, poor coordination, slurred speech
- **Moderate:** violent shivering, confusion, apathy, stumbling, poor judgement ("paradoxical undressing" — removing clothes because they feel hot)
- **Severe:** **shivering stops** — this is the critical danger sign, not an improvement — rigid muscles, slow weak pulse, shallow breathing, unconsciousness

**Treatment:**
1. **Get them out of the wind and off the ground.** The ground steals heat faster than the air. Put something insulating underneath — cardboard, a mat, dry leaves, a bag.
2. **Remove wet clothing** and replace with dry, or wrap in a dry blanket over the wet clothing if there is nothing dry.
3. **Insulate the whole body including the head and neck.** Most heat is lost from the head and the torso.
4. **Warm the core, not the limbs.** Warm packs or bottles at the chest, armpits, groin, and neck. **Do not rub the arms and legs** — this pushes cold blood back into the core and can stop the heart.
5. **Skin-to-skin contact inside a shared blanket** is highly effective.
6. **Warm sweet drinks only if fully conscious and able to swallow.** Tea with sugar is ideal.
7. **No alcohol.** It feels warming and it accelerates heat loss dangerously. Alcohol is involved in a large share of Indian cold deaths.
8. **Handle very gently.** In severe hypothermia, rough movement can trigger cardiac arrest.
9. **In severe hypothermia, "nobody is dead until they are warm and dead."** Continue CPR and warming — people have recovered fully from apparently absent vital signs.

**Frostbite** (Himalayan states, high passes, Ladakh):
- Signs: numbness, white or waxy skin, hard to the touch
- **Rewarm in water at 37-39 C** — comfortably warm to your elbow, never hot
- **Do not rub with snow, do not use direct heat, do not use a fire or a heater**
- **Do not rewarm if there is any chance of refreezing** — walking on a frozen foot is far better than walking on a thawed one that refreezes
- Do not burst blisters; get medical care

## Part 5: Surviving a Cold Wave

- **Layer clothing.** Several thin layers trap air and work far better than one thick one. The inner layer should not be wet cotton against the skin — cotton loses all insulation when damp.
- **Cover head, ears, neck, hands, and feet.** These lose heat fastest.
- **Stay dry.** Change out of wet clothes immediately.
- **Eat and drink.** The body needs fuel to generate heat. Hot food, hot drinks, and enough calories matter more than people realise.
- **Do not use a coal angithi, a charcoal sigri, or a kerosene heater in a closed room.** This is the classic Indian winter killer — **carbon monoxide poisoning kills whole families in their sleep** every winter in north India. If you must use one, keep a window open on two sides and never sleep with it burning.
- **Do not use an LPG stove or a generator indoors for heat.**
- **Do not drink alcohol to keep warm.**
- **Check on the elderly, the homeless, infants, and people living alone.** Night shelters (rain baseras) are run by municipal corporations in north Indian cities during cold waves; the numbers are published by the district administration.
- **Protect water pipes and livestock** in the hills.
- **Watch for exacerbations of heart and lung disease.** Cold weather increases heart attacks, strokes, and asthma attacks. Keep inhalers and cardiac medicines to hand.

# Desert Survival Guide

## Heat Management
The primary cause of death in the desert is hyperthermia and dehydration.
- **Travel at Night:** Movement during the heat of the day will drastically increase your body temperature and water loss. Travel only between dusk and dawn.
- **Keep Your Clothes On:** Do not remove clothing. Long sleeves and pants trap sweat against your skin, slowing evaporation and keeping you cooler while protecting your skin from severe sunburn.
- **Cover Your Head:** Protect your head and neck from direct sun exposure at all times.

## Finding Shade
- **Rest During the Day:** When the sun rises, stop moving immediately and find or build shade.
- **Natural Shade:** Look for overhangs, caves (check for snakes first), or large boulders.
- **Improvised Shade:** Use a space blanket, poncho, or extra clothing strung between two bushes or rocks. Ensure there is airflow beneath the shade.
- **Do Not Sit Directly on Sand:** The ground can be up to 30 degrees hotter than the air. Sit on a backpack, sleeping pad, or gathered brush to insulate yourself.

## Water Conservation
- **Do Not Ration Water:** If you have water, drink it when you are thirsty. "Rationing sweat, not water" is the rule. Drink water to stay hydrated; do not save it while you suffer heatstroke.
- **Breathe Through Your Nose:** Mouth breathing expels significantly more moisture from your body than breathing through your nose.
- **Do Not Eat:** Digestion requires massive amounts of water. If you have no water, do not consume food, especially protein.

## desert Signaling
- **Signal Mirrors:** A mirror or any highly reflective surface (CD, tin foil, polished metal) can flash sunlight at passing aircraft or vehicles miles away.
- **Smoke Signals:** During the day, add green vegetation, rubber, or plastic to a struggling fire to produce thick black smoke that contrasts with the desert sky.

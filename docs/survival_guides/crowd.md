# Crowd Crush and Stampede (India)

India has more mass-gathering crowd deaths than any other country. The recurring sites are religious gatherings (Kumbh Mela, temple festivals, Sabarimala, Vaishno Devi, Mandhradevi, Naina Devi, Ratangarh, Hathras), railway stations and platforms, political rallies and roadshows, free-distribution events, school and stadium exits, and narrow bridges and staircases.

**The word "stampede" is misleading.** People rarely die from being trampled by a panicking crowd. They die standing up, from **compressive asphyxia** — the pressure of bodies against their chest prevents them from expanding their ribcage to breathe. Death occurs in about 30 seconds to a few minutes, upright, often with no visible injury.

Understanding this changes what you do.

---

## Part 1: Reading Crowd Density Before It Is Too Late

Density is measured in people per square metre. Learn what each feels like.

| Density | What it feels like | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 per m2 | You can walk freely, choose your direction | Safe |
| 3 per m2 | You brush against others, walking is slowed | Caution |
| **4 per m2** | **You are touching people on all sides. You cannot change direction freely.** | **Danger threshold — leave now** |
| 5 per m2 | You cannot raise your arms to your face. Movement happens in waves. | Critical |
| 6+ per m2 | You are lifted off your feet by the crowd. You cannot breathe deeply. | Lethal |

**The single rule: the moment you cannot freely put your hands to your face or take a step in the direction you choose, leave immediately and sideways.** Do not wait to see whether it gets worse. Density can go from 4 to 6 in under a minute and there is no exit above 5.

**Other warning signs:**
- You feel shockwaves or ripples travelling through the crowd — this means pressure is being transmitted, and the crowd is behaving like a fluid
- You hear shouting, screaming, or a sudden change in the crowd's sound
- The crowd is moving in two directions at once, or into a bottleneck
- Involuntary movement — you are being carried
- The temperature and humidity rise sharply and you cannot smell fresh air
- Barriers, gates, or a narrowing lane ahead

## Part 2: Before You Enter a Crowd

Ten seconds of preparation, every single time.

1. **Identify at least two exits when you arrive** — not the one everyone came in through. Note where the edges are, where there is a wall you can get behind, and where there is higher ground.
2. **Stay near the edges and near the back.** The centre of a crowd has no escape. Ninety percent of the risk is in the middle.
3. **Never enter a crowd through a bottleneck** — a gate, a tunnel, a staircase, a bridge, a narrow lane. Bottlenecks are where people die.
4. **Do not carry a child on your shoulders in dense crowd.** In a surge, the child falls into the crush. Carry a small child **on your chest, held high, facing you**, so your body shields them and you can lift them above the compression zone.
5. **Agree a rally point outside the venue with everyone you came with**, and a time. Assume phones will not work — networks always fail at large Indian gatherings.
6. **Wear closed, laced shoes.** Chappals and slip-ons come off, and bare feet in a crowd end in serious injury.
7. **Do not carry a bag on your back or a long strap across your body.** It can be pulled or caught. Wear it on the front if you must carry one.
8. **Do not take a stroller or a wheelchair into a dense area** unless there is a marked accessible route.

## Part 3: If You Are Caught in a Crush

Everything here goes against instinct. Practise thinking it now so you can do it then.

**1. Do not fight the crowd. Move with it.**
Pushing against the flow burns energy you desperately need and achieves nothing. Let it carry you and use the momentum.

**2. Get your arms up in front of your chest — the "boxer" position.**
Hands in fists or palms together in front of your sternum, elbows out, forearms vertical. This creates a rigid cage around your ribcage and preserves 5-10 cm of space in front of your chest. **That space is the difference between breathing and asphyxiating.** Keep your elbows out to hold the space. This is the single most important physical action in this guide.

**3. Breathe out only when the pressure eases.**
Take breaths in the gaps between surges. Do not exhale fully during a compression — you may not be able to inhale again.

**4. Move diagonally, in the gaps, during the lulls.**
Crowd pressure comes in waves. Between waves, there is a moment of slack. Use each lull to take one or two diagonal steps towards the edge. Do not try to move straight across the flow; move at an angle with it. Repeat with every lull. This is how you get out — slowly, sideways, over minutes.

**5. Keep your feet under you.**
Stay upright at all costs. Do not bend down for anything. Not a phone, not a shoe, not a bag, not money. If you go down in a crush you may not get up.

**6. If you fall:**
- **Get up immediately** if there is any gap at all. Use anything solid to push against.
- If you cannot get up: **curl onto your side in the foetal position, knees to chest, arms over your head, elbows out** to protect your ribcage. Do not lie face down (you cannot expand your chest against the ground) and do not lie face up (your chest is exposed to feet).
- **Grab someone's leg and pull yourself up** at the first opportunity. Shout "I am getting up, help me."

**7. Help someone who has fallen.**
If you can, form a ring facing outward around a fallen person and hold the space open. A crowd will often respect a shouted, clear instruction: "STOP! SOMEONE IS DOWN! STEP BACK!" Do this with your arms up.

**8. Stay away from walls, barriers, and fixed objects.**
Pressure builds against anything solid. Being pinned against a wall or a barricade is more lethal than being in the open flow. Never let yourself be pushed into a corner.

## Part 4: If You Are With Children or Elderly

- **Small child: hold them high on your chest, facing you, with their head above your shoulder.** Your forearms make the cage; their body is inside it.
- **Older child: hold their wrist, not their hand.** Hands get pulled apart. A wrist grip does not slip.
- **Brief them before you enter**: "If we get separated, stand still where you are and shout my name. Do not run to find me. I will come to you." Tell them to go to a uniformed person, a shop, or a fixed landmark you have both identified.
- **Write your phone number on the child's forearm in pen** before entering a large gathering. This is standard practice at Kumbh and it works.
- **Elderly or frail people should not be in dense crowd at all.** If you must, keep them at the edge, near an exit, and leave early — always leave before the event ends, never with the crowd.

## Part 5: Specific Indian Situations

**Religious gatherings and temple queues:** the danger points are the narrow approach corridors, the darshan queue barricades, the exit after the deity, and the bridges and stairs. Rumours ("the bridge is collapsing", "there is a snake", "the gate is closing") have triggered several of India's deadliest crushes. **Do not react to a rumour by running. Move sideways and calmly.**

**Railway platforms and foot overbridges:** the classic Indian pattern is a last-minute platform-change announcement sending thousands across a narrow FOB in two directions. **Never board a moving train, never run on a foot overbridge, and if the FOB is packed, wait — you will miss the train and live.** Stand well behind the yellow line; a passing train's slipstream can pull you in.

**Free distribution, ration, and relief queues:** these kill regularly. Stay at the back, arrive early or late but never at the announced opening time, and leave if the crowd is pushing.

**Political rallies and roadshows:** the crush is usually at the exit when the leader leaves. Leave 15 minutes before the end.

**Concerts and stadiums:** the front-of-stage barrier area and the exit tunnels are the danger zones. Never go into the pit if you are small, alone, or with a child.

**Fire in a crowded place:** the crowd is now the bigger threat. Go to the exit **nearest to you, not the one you came in by** — the entrance is where everyone else will go. Stay low, stay at the edges, and keep moving.

## Part 6: After a Crush

- **Casualties will look uninjured.** Compressive asphyxia leaves no wounds. Check breathing and consciousness in anyone who is sitting down, dazed, or lying still.
- **Blue or grey lips and face, petechiae (tiny red spots) on the face and eyes, and confusion** are signs of asphyxia. Move them to open air, sit them up or lay them on their side, loosen everything around the chest and neck.
- **Start CPR** on anyone not breathing. See the **Medical** guide, Part 3.
- **Expect crush injuries and rib fractures.** Handle carefully.
- **Do not move the injured into the crowd flow.** Establish a casualty point at the edge, against a wall, and mark it.
- **Send someone to bring help physically.** Networks are always down at these events.
- **Register at the hospital or police desk** so your family can find you.

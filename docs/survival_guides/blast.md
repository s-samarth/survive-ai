# Bomb Blasts, Terror Attacks and Active Shooters (India)

India has faced sustained terrorist attack on civilian targets: markets, trains and railway stations, buses, hotels, temples, mosques, courts, and crowded public spaces. The threat is real in metros, in border states, in Jammu and Kashmir, in the North East, and in Naxal-affected districts of central and eastern India.

This guide covers explosions, gunmen, and hostage situations. The core principle in all three is the same: **distance and cover, in that order, immediately.**

---

## Part 1: Recognising a Threat Before It Detonates

**Suspicious objects** — an unattended bag, tiffin box, cycle, scooter, or parcel in a crowded place, especially one that:
- Is in an odd location (under a seat, behind a pillar, in a bin, against a load-bearing column)
- Has wires, a protruding antenna, a switch, or tape
- Is unusually heavy for its size, or leaking, or smells of chemicals or almonds
- Is abandoned and nobody claims it after an announcement

**What to do:**
1. **Do not touch it, move it, or open it. Do not cover it.**
2. **Do not use a mobile phone or a walkie-talkie within 30 metres.** Radio signals can trigger some devices.
3. **Move away and take others with you.** 100 metres minimum for a small package; 300+ metres for a vehicle.
4. **Move at right angles and put solid structure between you and it.**
5. **Inform police (100/112) or railway/metro security from a safe distance.**
6. **Do not gather to look.** Secondary devices are specifically placed to kill the crowd that gathers and the responders who arrive.

**Suspicious behaviour** — wearing bulky or out-of-season clothing, excessive sweating and nervousness, repeatedly checking a bag, unusual interest in security arrangements, muttering, or an abandoned vehicle parked awkwardly against a building.

## Part 2: If a Blast Occurs

**In the first seconds:**
1. **Drop flat, face down, feet towards the blast, mouth open, hands over ears and head.** Mouth open equalises pressure and protects the eardrums and lungs. Feet towards the blast presents the smallest target to the pressure wave and fragments.
2. **Stay down until debris stops falling.** The blast wave is followed by fragments, then falling glass and masonry.
3. **Then move, low and fast, away from the seat of the blast, keeping structure between you and it.**

**Immediately after:**
- **Assume a second device.** This is standard terrorist practice worldwide and has been used repeatedly in India. **Move away and keep moving.** Do not stop at the obvious assembly point, the car park, or the road outside — these are exactly where secondary devices are placed.
- **Do not run towards the blast** to help. Get clear first, then assess.
- **Avoid the area around vehicles, bins, and unattended bags** as you leave.
- **Get out of the building if it is structurally damaged.** Blast weakens structures which then collapse.
- **Do not use the lift.**
- **Do not use your phone near the scene** until you are well clear.

**Blast injuries you may not see:**
- **Blast lung** — the pressure wave damages lung tissue with no external wound. Signs: breathlessness, coughing blood, chest pain, developing over minutes to hours. **Anyone close to a blast needs medical assessment even if they look unhurt.**
- **Ruptured eardrums** — deafness or ringing. A marker that the person was close enough to have internal injuries.
- **Abdominal injury** — the gas-filled bowel is vulnerable. Belly pain, tenderness, or rigidity after a blast is serious.
- **Penetrating fragment wounds** — small entry holes that bleed internally. Look for them.
- **Traumatic amputation and severe bleeding** — see the **Medical** guide, Part 2. Tourniquets save lives in blast injuries.

## Part 3: Active Shooter — Run, Hide, Fight

The sequence is in priority order. Do not skip to a later step if an earlier one is available.

**RUN**
- **If there is a safe escape route, go, immediately, and do not wait for others to agree.** Hesitation kills.
- **Leave everything behind.** Bags, phones on the table, shoes, all of it.
- **Do not stop to film.**
- **Move away from the sound of gunfire**, not towards it. Use cover as you go — walls, pillars, vehicles.
- **Keep your hands visible and empty** as you exit, especially when you meet police — armed responders cannot tell you from an attacker.
- **Stop others from entering the area.**

**HIDE**
- **Get out of the shooter's line of sight**, into a room, and **lock and barricade the door** with furniture.
- **Turn off the lights. Silence your phone completely** — silent mode, not vibrate; vibration on a hard surface is audible.
- **Get away from the door and away from windows.** Lie flat behind something solid. **Understand the difference between cover (stops bullets — concrete, engine block, thick masonry) and concealment (only hides you — drywall, plywood, glass, a car door).**
- **Stay silent and stay still.**
- **Do not open the door for anyone**, including a voice claiming to be police, until you can verify. Police will identify themselves and instruct you clearly.
- **If you can, text emergency services and a family member** with your exact location, number of people, and what you know. Text, do not call — a ringing phone or a whispered call gives you away.

**FIGHT**
- **Only as an absolute last resort, when your life is in immediate danger.**
- **Commit completely.** Act aggressively, act together with everyone in the room, use anything as a weapon — a fire extinguisher, a chair, a rod, boiling water — and aim for the face and the weapon.
- **Attack the moment they enter or reload.**

**When police arrive:**
- **Keep your hands empty, open, and visible above your head.**
- **Do not run at them, do not grab them, do not point.**
- **Follow instructions exactly and immediately.** Expect to be searched and treated as a suspect — the first units are there to neutralise the threat, not to help the injured. Rescue teams follow.
- Tell them: location and number of shooters, description, weapon type, number of casualties, and where they are.

## Part 4: Hostage Situations

- **Do the first 15 minutes right.** This is when hostage-takers are most agitated and most people are killed. Be still, be quiet, be compliant.
- **Do not make eye contact, do not stare, do not challenge, do not argue.**
- **Do not try to be a hero and do not try to negotiate on behalf of the group.** Both mark you out.
- **Do not draw attention** — no sudden movements, no whispering, no phone use unless you are certain you are unobserved.
- **Follow instructions.** Hand over valuables without hesitation.
- **Be a person, quietly.** If addressed, respond calmly and briefly. Being seen as a human being rather than a category slightly improves survival odds, but do not force conversation.
- **Observe and remember**: number of attackers, weapons, accents, languages, names used, layout, other hostages, exits.
- **Eat and drink when offered.** Rest when you can. You may need endurance.
- **During a rescue assault: get on the floor immediately, face down, hands visible on your head, and stay there.** Do not run, do not stand, do not move towards the rescuers. Rescue teams enter fast and shoot at anyone standing or moving. Stay down until you are physically moved.

---

## Part 5: Vehicle-Borne and Ramming Attacks

- **In a crowded pedestrian area, keep a barrier or a solid structure between you and the roadway** where you can — bollards, planters, a parked heavy vehicle.
- **If a vehicle is driven at a crowd: move perpendicular to its path, not along it.** Get behind something solid and heavy.
- **Do not stand in the open in the vehicle's line of travel to film.**

---

## Part 6: Chemical, Biological or Radiological Suspicion

If a blast is accompanied by an unusual smell, a coloured vapour, a fine powder, or many people suddenly collapsing with no visible injuries:

- **Move upwind and uphill immediately, and get to fresh air.**
- **Do not run towards casualties.**
- **Cover your nose and mouth with cloth**, hold your breath while moving out if the cloud is visible.
- **Remove contaminated clothing** by cutting it off rather than pulling it over your head, bag it, and wash with soap and lots of water.
- See the **Chemical and Industrial** guide.

## Part 7: Trains, Stations and Metros

India's rail network is a repeated target.

- **On a train, if there is a blast or fire in your coach**, move to the vestibule and into the next coach away from it. Use the emergency window (marked with a coloured stripe) and the hammer beside it if the doors are blocked.
- **Pull the alarm chain only when it is safe to stop** — not on a bridge, in a tunnel, or in a remote stretch if there is an attacker on board.
- **On a platform, move away from the platform edge and off the platform** — do not jump onto the tracks unless directed; overhead lines carry 25,000 volts and a train may be approaching.
- **In a metro, use the passenger emergency intercom** in each coach, and follow staff instructions. Do not force the doors between stations.
- **Report unattended baggage to RPF/GRP immediately.** "Lawaris saman" announcements exist for a reason.

---

## Part 8: Afterwards

- **Get away from the scene and stay away.** Secondary devices, structural collapse, and gas leaks all follow.
- **Register yourself at a hospital or the police desk** even if uninjured, so you are removed from casualty lists.
- **Seek medical assessment if you were anywhere near a blast**, for blast lung and hearing damage.
- **Expect severe psychological effects** — this is normal and it is treatable. Flashbacks, sleeplessness, hypervigilance, and avoidance for weeks are typical. Persistent symptoms beyond a month need help. Tele-MANAS (**14416**) is India's free national mental health helpline and operates in multiple Indian languages.
- **Do not spread unverified casualty figures or accusations.** Rumour after a terror attack in India frequently causes communal violence, which then kills more people than the attack did.

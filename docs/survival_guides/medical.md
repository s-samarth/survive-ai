# Medical Survival Guide

## Tourniquet Application
Use for severe, life-threatening arterial bleeding on limbs:
1. **Position High and Tight:** Place the tourniquet 2-3 inches above the bleeding wound. Do not place it over a joint (go above the joint if necessary).
2. **Tighten Securely:** Tighten the strap, then turn the windlass (the rod) until the bleeding completely stops.
3. **Secure the Windlass:** Lock the rod into the clip built into the tourniquet.
4. **Note the Time:** Write the time of application directly on the tourniquet or the patient's forehead (e.g., "T: 14:30").
5. **Never Remove:** Once applied, do not loosen or remove the tourniquet. Only medical professionals should do this.

## Wound Packing
For deep wounds where a tourniquet cannot be applied (e.g., groin, armpit, neck):
1. **Find the Source:** Quickly wipe away pooled blood to identify exactly where the bleeding is coming from inside the wound.
2. **Pack Tightly:** Shove hemostatic gauze (or clean cloth if unavailable) deep into the wound directly against the bleeding vessel. Keep packing until the wound cavity is full.
3. **Apply Direct Pressure:** Press down hard with both hands on top of the packed wound for at least 3 minutes (or 10 minutes for standard non-hemostatic gauze).

## Treating Shock
Shock is a critical drop in blood flow that can follow severe injury:
- **Lay the Patient Flat:** Keep them supine to maximize blood flow to the brain and vital organs.
- **Elevate Legs:** If there are no leg fractures or spinal injuries, elevate their feet 6-12 inches.
- **Keep Them Warm:** Cover the patient with a blanket or jacket to prevent heat loss.
- **Do Not Give Fluids:** Even if they complain of thirst, do not give them anything to eat or drink, as they may need surgery or could choke.

## Fracture Splinting
To stabilize a broken bone and reduce pain/tissue damage:
- **Do Not Realign:** Do not try to push bones back into place. Splint the limb in the position you found it.
- **Pad the Splint:** Use soft clothing or padding between the rigid splint (sticks, rolled magazines, boards) and the limb.
- **Secure Above and Below:** Tie the splint snugly (but not restricting blood flow) above and below the fractured joint/bone.

## Severe Dehydration
- **Symptoms:** Extreme thirst, dry mouth, lack of sweating, dark urine, confusion, dizziness.
- **Treatment:** Move to a cool, shaded area. 
- **Sip, Don't Gulp:** Give water in small sips rather than large gulps to prevent vomiting.
- **Electrolytes:** If available, mix oral rehydration salts into the water to replace lost minerals.

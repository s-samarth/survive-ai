# Fire — Homes, High-Rises, Slums and LPG (India)

Fire is one of the most common causes of unnatural death in India. The specific Indian patterns are: LPG cylinder incidents in kitchens, saree and dupatta catching a gas flame, electrical short circuits from overloaded and unregulated wiring, kerosene stoves, chawl and slum fires that spread through dozens of homes in minutes, and commercial buildings with blocked or locked fire exits.

**Smoke kills before flame does.** Most fire deaths are from carbon monoxide and toxic smoke inhalation, often before the person even wakes up.

---

## Part 1: The First Thirty Seconds

1. **Shout "AAG! AAG!" loudly and continuously** as you move. Waking the building is more valuable than anything else you can do.
2. **Get everyone out.** People first. Nothing else.
3. **Feel every door with the back of your hand before opening it** — the back of the hand, so a burn does not disable your grip. Check the door, the handle, and the gap at the top. **If it is hot, do not open it.** Fire is on the other side and opening it will bring a flashover into your face.
4. **Stay low.** The air near the floor is cooler and has breathable oxygen; at standing height it may be 300 C and lethal in a breath. **Crawl on hands and knees, or on your belly.**
5. **Cover your nose and mouth with cloth, wet if possible.** A wet cloth filters particulates and cools the air. It does not filter carbon monoxide — it buys minutes, not immunity.
6. **Close every door behind you.** A closed wooden door holds back fire and smoke for 20-30 minutes. This single habit saves more lives in high-rise fires than anything else.
7. **Never use the lift.** It will stop at the fire floor, or lose power with you inside.
8. **Once out, stay out.** Do not go back for anything or anyone — tell the fire service who is still inside and where.

---

## Part 2: If You Cannot Get Out

Sometimes the stairs are impassable. This is survivable if you do it correctly.

1. **Go into a room with a window, preferably on the side away from the fire.** Close the door.
2. **Seal the door.** Stuff wet cloth, bedsheets, or towels into the gap at the bottom and around the edges. Tape or wet cloth over vents and AC ducts.
3. **Open the window** for air — but **close it again if smoke starts coming in from outside**, which happens when fire climbs the facade.
4. **Signal from the window**: a torch, a bright cloth, waving. Shout only when you can see someone. Conserve your voice and your air.
5. **Call 101 or 112 and tell them exactly which floor and which room you are in**, and how many people. Do this even if you already called once. Fire crews prioritise confirmed locations.
6. **Fill the bathtub and buckets** with water. Wet the door and the floor around it.
7. **Do not jump from above the second floor.** A jump from the third floor or higher is usually fatal or crippling. Firefighters reach higher floors; a fall does not become survivable. Wait, seal, and signal.
8. **Do not hide in a cupboard, under a bed, or in a bathroom with no window.** Children do this and rescuers cannot find them. Teach children to go to a window and shout.

---

## Part 3: If Your Clothes Catch Fire

**Stop, Drop, Roll.** Do not run — running feeds the flame with oxygen and drives it towards your face.

- **Stop** moving immediately.
- **Drop** to the ground.
- **Cover your face with your hands.**
- **Roll** back and forth to smother the flames.

If you are helping someone else, smother them with a heavy blanket, a durrie, a jacket, or a thick cotton sheet — **not a synthetic or nylon fabric, which melts onto skin.** Once out, cool the burn with running water for 20 minutes. See the **Medical** guide, Part 6.

**Saree and dupatta fires** are a specific and very common Indian kitchen injury. Synthetic sarees ignite fast and burn hot. Tuck loose ends before cooking, and keep a bucket of water or a wet cloth accessible in every kitchen.

## Part 4: LPG Cylinder Emergencies

**If you smell gas:**
1. **Do not touch any electrical switch — on or off.** The spark from a switch is a common ignition source. Do not turn on a light, a fan, an exhaust, or a doorbell. Do not use a phone in the room.
2. **Do not light a match, a lighter, or an agarbatti.**
3. **Open all doors and windows** to ventilate.
4. **Turn off the regulator** on the cylinder — turn the knob to the OFF position and remove the regulator if you can.
5. **Take the cylinder outside** into the open if it is safe to carry, and keep it upright.
6. **Evacuate and call the LPG emergency helpline 1906**, and 101 for fire.

**If the cylinder or the regulator is on fire:**
- A cylinder with a jet of flame at the valve is **less** dangerous than a cylinder heating in a fire with the valve closed. A burning jet is releasing pressure.
- **If you can safely reach the regulator, turn it off.** Approach from upwind, with a wet cloth over your hands.
- **If the cylinder is inside a fire, get everyone out to at least 100 metres and call 101.** A BLEVE (boiling liquid expanding vapour explosion) will destroy the building. Do not try to move a hot cylinder.
- **Keep neighbouring cylinders cool** with water from a distance if you can do so with cover.

**Prevention:** check the rubber tube every 6 months (it has an expiry date printed), use only ISI-marked regulators and tubes, keep the cylinder upright and below the stove, never use a cylinder in an enclosed room with no ventilation, and turn the regulator off every night.

## Part 5: Electrical Fires and Short Circuits

Indian domestic wiring is frequently overloaded — high-draw appliances (geyser, AC, iron, induction) on circuits designed decades ago, extension boards daisy-chained, and aluminium wiring in older buildings.

- **Never throw water on an electrical fire.** Water conducts, and you will be electrocuted.
- **Cut the power at the mains first**, if you can reach the board safely and your hands and feet are dry.
- **Smother a small electrical fire** with a CO2 or dry powder extinguisher (ABC type), sand, or a heavy non-synthetic blanket.
- **Warning signs of impending electrical fire**: a burning plastic smell, warm or discoloured switch plates, sparking sockets, frequently tripping MCBs, flickering lights when a heavy appliance starts, and buzzing from the distribution board. **Act on these.** Get an electrician.
- **Do not use a socket that is warm.** Do not run a geyser or AC through an extension board.
- **Keep an ABC dry powder extinguisher in the kitchen and near the electrical board.** Check the pressure gauge every 6 months.

## Part 6: Slum, Chawl and Dense-Settlement Fires

These spread with terrifying speed — tarpaulin, plastic sheeting, tightly packed structures, narrow lanes, illegal power connections, and no fire tender access.

- **The lane is the escape route and it will be blocked within a minute.** Move immediately and move outward, not along the fire front.
- **Do not stop to collect anything.** These fires cross 20 homes in 3 minutes.
- **Get to open ground, a main road, or a water body** and account for children.
- **Create a firebreak** if you have the time and numbers: pull down or wet the structures in the fire's path ahead of it. This is how communities have actually stopped these fires before tenders arrived.
- **Warn upwind neighbours by running, not by phone.**
- **After: watch for cylinder explosions.** Every home has an LPG cylinder and they cook off for hours.

---

## Part 7: Public Buildings, Cinemas, Malls and Trains

- **Whenever you enter any enclosed public space, look for two exits.** This costs 5 seconds and it is the single highest-value safety habit there is. In Indian commercial buildings, one exit is very often locked, blocked with stock, or leads to a dead end.
- **In a cinema or auditorium:** count the rows between you and the exit, so you can find it in darkness and smoke.
- **If an alarm sounds, leave.** Do not wait to see if it is real. Indian fire deaths in commercial buildings are dominated by people who waited for confirmation.
- **In a train:** know that the emergency window (marked with a yellow or green stripe) has a hammer beside it. In a fire, move to the vestibule and to the next coach, away from the fire; do not pull the chain in a tunnel or on a bridge.
- **In a metro:** use the passenger emergency intercom, do not force the doors between stations, do not get onto the tracks unless staff direct you — the third rail carries lethal current.

---

## Part 8: Smoke Inhalation

Anyone who has been in smoke needs watching for 24 hours, even if they look fine.

**Danger signs:** soot around the nose or mouth, singed nasal hairs or eyebrows, a hoarse or changed voice, a persistent cough, stridor (a harsh noise on breathing in), difficulty breathing, confusion, headache, or cherry-red skin.

**Treatment:** move to fresh air, sit them upright, loosen clothing, give oxygen if available, and get to a hospital. **Airway swelling from hot smoke can close the throat hours after the fire.** Carbon monoxide poisoning causes headache, nausea, and confusion and cannot be detected by smell.

---

## Part 9: Fire Prevention at Home

- **Working smoke alarm** — cheap, battery-powered, and almost absent from Indian homes. Fit one outside each sleeping area. Test monthly.
- **ABC dry powder extinguisher** in the kitchen and near the electrical board. Learn PASS: **P**ull the pin, **A**im at the base of the fire, **S**queeze, **S**weep.
- **Fire blanket** in the kitchen.
- **Never leave cooking unattended.** Never pour water on a burning oil pan — slide a lid or a thali over it and turn off the gas.
- **Do not overload sockets or daisy-chain extension boards.**
- **Do not charge phones, laptops, or e-scooters overnight on a bed or a sofa**, and use only the original charger. Lithium battery fires in Indian homes are rising sharply and they burn fiercely, cannot be extinguished with water once in thermal runaway, and release toxic fumes.
- **Keep the escape route clear.** Do not store anything on the staircase or in the passage. Do not fit a grill or a lock on a window that cannot be opened from the inside — window grills are the most common reason Indian families die trapped in their own flats. If you must have grills, fit at least one openable panel per floor and keep the key hanging beside it.
- **Practise the escape route with children**, including in the dark, twice a year. Agree a rally point outside.

# War, Air Raids and Border Conflict (India)

This guide covers the civilian survival situation in a war or a serious border conflict: air raids, drones, missiles, shelling, blackout orders, and life in a contested or occupied area. India's border regions — Jammu and Kashmir, Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat's Kutch, Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim — plus military and industrial cities anywhere in the country, are the areas of concern.

India has a Civil Defence organisation, air raid warning systems, and mock drills conducted by state authorities. Learn your local siren signals now.

---

## Part 1: Air Raid Warnings

Indian civil defence siren signals:
- **A rising and falling wailing tone for 2 minutes = ALERT / RED WARNING.** Attack is imminent or in progress. Take cover immediately.
- **A steady continuous tone for 2 minutes = ALL CLEAR.** The raid is over.
- Some areas also use a **short intermittent tone for a "caution" or "long all clear"** state. Confirm your local convention with the district civil defence office.

**When the siren sounds:**
1. **Take cover immediately.** You have seconds to a couple of minutes.
2. **Turn off gas at the regulator and electricity at the main** if you can do it in passing.
3. **Do not go outside to look. Do not film.**
4. **Do not use the lift.**
5. **Stay in cover until the all-clear**, not until the noise stops. Raids come in waves.

---

## Part 2: Where to Shelter

**Best to worst, in a normal Indian building:**

1. **A basement or an underground parking level**, away from the walls, under a solid beam
2. **A ground-floor interior room with no exterior wall** — a bathroom, a storeroom, or a stairwell core. Concrete stair cores are the strongest part of most Indian RCC buildings.
3. **A corridor on a low floor, away from all windows**
4. **Under a heavy table** in an interior room, against an interior wall, with mattresses over you
5. **A trench outside**, if you are in the open and there is nothing else. Lie face down, feet towards the blast, mouth open, hands over ears.

**Avoid:** top floors, rooms with exterior windows, anywhere near glass, lifts, stairwells with glass, balconies, and the space directly under a water tank.

**The two-wall rule:** get at least two solid walls between you and the outside. Fragments and glass, not the direct hit, cause most casualties.

**Prepare a shelter room now:**
- Choose the room. Clear it.
- Store water (3 litres per person per day, 5 days), dry food, ORS, a torch, a battery radio, a first aid kit, medicines, a whistle, and a bucket with a lid for sanitation
- Keep documents, cash, and a change of clothes in a bag by the door
- Tape or board the windows in adjacent rooms; the tape holds shards together
- Keep helmets or thick cushions for every person's head
- Know where the gas and electrical mains are

## Part 3: Under Shelling or Artillery

- **Shelling comes in salvos with pauses.** Do not move during a salvo. Use the pause between salvos to move to better cover, and be back in cover before the next one.
- **Stay low.** Most fragments travel outward and slightly upward. Lying flat dramatically increases survival.
- **Get below ground level if you can** — a basement, a trench, a drainage ditch, a culvert.
- **If caught in the open with no cover, lie flat in the lowest depression available, feet towards the fire, mouth open, hands over your ears and head.**
- **Do not shelter behind a vehicle** — fuel and the vehicle itself are targets and fragments punch straight through sheet metal.
- **Do not shelter in a doorway or against an outside wall** of a building being shelled.
- **After a strike, wait.** Follow-up strikes on the same location minutes later are common.

---

## Part 4: Drones and Loitering Munitions

- **A small drone overhead is reconnaissance, targeting, or armed.** Assume you are being observed.
- **Get under overhead cover** — a roof, a tree canopy, a bridge, a vehicle. Thermal and optical sensors both need line of sight.
- **Do not gather in groups in the open.** Groups are targets. Disperse and move separately.
- **At night, use no white light.** A torch, a phone screen, a cigarette, or a vehicle headlight is visible for kilometres to a thermal or night-vision sensor.
- **Do not shoot at, throw things at, or approach a downed drone.** It may be armed and may be command-detonated.
- **Do not touch any unfamiliar object** dropped from a drone.

---

## Part 5: Blackout Discipline

Blackout orders are issued during air raid alerts and drills in Indian border areas.

- **Switch off all exterior and interior lights** visible from outside.
- **Cover windows completely** — thick curtains, blankets, cardboard, black plastic, newspaper. Check from outside that no light escapes.
- **Cover vehicle headlights** partially with tape or paper if you must drive, and drive slowly with the minimum light.
- **No open flames visible from outside.** No phone screens at windows.
- **Turn off the inverter's tell-tale LEDs, streetside signage, and shop lights.**
- **Do not use a torch outdoors.** If you must, use a red filter, point it at the ground, and shield it with your hand.

## Part 6: Unexploded Ordnance and Landmines

Relevant in border districts, in areas that have been shelled, and along the LoC and IB.

**The rule: if you did not put it there, do not touch it.**

- **Unexploded shells, bomblets, submunitions, rockets, and grenades** may look inert, rusted, harmless, or like a toy or a metal ball. They are not.
- **Do not touch, move, kick, throw, burn, or sell for scrap.** Scrap collection of ordnance kills children and labourers in Indian border districts every year.
- **Mark the location** with stones, cloth, or a stick from a safe distance and report it to the police, the army, or the district administration.
- **Keep children away and tell them explicitly** what these look like and never to touch anything metal they find.

**If you are in a suspected mined area:**
- **Stop. Do not move.** Look at the ground around you.
- **Retrace your exact footsteps back the way you came**, stepping precisely in your own prints.
- If you cannot see your own prints, stay where you are and call for help.
- **Stay on hard-surfaced, well-used roads and paths.** Mines are placed on shoulders, verges, shortcuts, and in likely stopping places.
- **Warning signs of mined areas:** red-painted stones or red triangles, skull-and-crossbones signs, fencing, dead livestock, abandoned fields adjacent to cultivated ones, and areas villagers avoid without explanation. **Ask locals — they always know.**

---

## Part 7: Checkpoints and Armed Personnel

- **Approach slowly, hands visible and empty.** At night, put the interior light on in a vehicle and slow down early.
- **Do not make sudden movements. Do not reach into a bag or a pocket without saying what you are doing first.**
- **Carry ID at all times.** Aadhaar, and any residence or permit document required in your area.
- **Answer questions briefly and factually.** Do not argue, do not joke, do not film, do not ask for names or numbers.
- **Do not photograph checkpoints, military installations, bridges, or convoys.** This is an offence and will be treated as hostile.
- **Follow the vehicle in front at a distance** and do not overtake a military convoy.
- **If detained,** stay calm, comply, and ask to inform a family member. Note names, times, and the unit if you safely can.

---

## Part 8: Evacuation and Displacement

- **Go early.** Roads close, fuel runs out, and trains fill. Families who wait for certainty get trapped.
- **Follow the official evacuation route** if one is designated; unofficial routes may be mined, shelled, or blocked.
- **Travel in daylight** where possible, and in a group.
- **Take:** ID for every person including children, cash in small notes, water, ORS, dry food, medicines and prescriptions, phone and powerbank, torch, warm clothing, and a written list of contacts.
- **Mark your house** on departure: names, number of people, destination, date.
- **Register at the relief camp** or with the district administration on arrival.
- **Keep the family together and keep children physically attached to an adult.** Family separation is the single largest harm in displacement.
- **Do not carry anything that could be read as a weapon or as partisan material.**

---

## Part 9: Water, Food and Health in a Prolonged Conflict

- **Water is the first thing to fail.** Fill everything at the first warning. Assume the supply will be contaminated or cut. Boil or chlorinate everything. See the **Water, Food and Sanitation** guide.
- **Food:** stock non-perishable, no-cook items. Ration from day one, not from the day you notice supplies running low.
- **Medicines:** chronic patients — diabetics, cardiac, dialysis, TB, epilepsy, psychiatric — are at the greatest risk in a prolonged conflict, not the wounded. **Get 30 days of medication in advance** and keep the prescription on paper.
- **Sanitation:** a covered bucket with a lid, ash or lime, and a designated disposal point away from water. Disease kills far more civilians in prolonged conflicts than weapons do.
- **Do not gather in large groups**, including at water points, markets, and relief distributions, for longer than necessary.
- **Keep a battery radio.** All India Radio is the official information channel and it will keep broadcasting.

## Part 10: Nuclear or Radiological Event

Extremely unlikely, but the protective actions are simple and worth knowing.

- **If you see an intense flash, do not look at it.** Drop flat, face down, feet towards it, hands over your head and ears, mouth open. Stay down for at least a minute — the blast wave follows the flash.
- **Then get inside, immediately, and get as deep and central as you can.** A basement or the middle of a large concrete building. Distance and mass are the only protection.
- **Stay inside for at least 24 hours, preferably 48.** Fallout radiation decays extremely fast — it drops by roughly 90% in the first 7 hours and 99% in about 2 days. Going outside early is far more dangerous than staying put without food.
- **Remove outer clothing** if you were outside — this removes most contamination — and bag it away from you. **Shower with soap and lukewarm water**, no conditioner (it binds particles to hair). Blow your nose and wipe your eyelids and ears.
- **Seal the room** — close windows and doors, switch off AC and exhaust fans, tape gaps.
- **Drink only bottled or sealed water and food from sealed containers.** Food inside a sealed packet or a fridge is safe.
- **Potassium iodide (KI)** protects only the thyroid, only from radioactive iodine, and only if taken at the right time. **Take it only when authorities instruct you to.** Do not take it speculatively, do not exceed the dose, and do not use iodine antiseptic or iodised salt as a substitute — that is dangerous and useless.
- **Listen to All India Radio for instructions.**

# General Survival Guide

## Fire Starting
Fire provides warmth, water purification, signaling capability, and psychological comfort.
- **Gather Preparation:** Before striking a spark, gather tinder (dry grass, dryer lint, birch bark), kindling (small dry twigs), and fuel (increasingly larger dry wood).
- **The Teepee Method:** Build a small cone of kindling around your tinder nest.
- **Ignition:** Use waterproof matches, a lighter, or a ferrocerium rod to ignite the tinder.
- **Protect the Flame:** Build a small windbreak out of rocks or logs to prevent the initial spark from blowing out.

## Signaling for Rescue
- **The Rule of Three:** The international distress signal is three of anything: three fires in a triangle, three loud blasts on a whistle, or three gunshots.
- **Ground-to-Air Signals:** 
  - Need Help: Make a large "V" shape on the ground.
  - Need Medical: Make a large "X" shape.
  - Make the symbols out of highly contrasting materials (dark rocks on white snow, bright clothing on green grass) and make them as massive as physically possible.
- **Whistle:** Blow a whistle vigorously. The sound of a whistle travels substantially farther through trees than the human voice.

## Survival Psychology
The mind is the most critical survival tool.
- **S.T.O.P. Principle:**
  - **S**top: When you realize you are lost or in danger, immediately halt. Do not wander.
  - **T**hink: Consider your resources, the time of day, and the weather.
  - **O**bserve: Look around for immediate threats, shelter locations, and landmarks.
  - **P**lan: Formulate a deliberate action plan before taking a single step.
- **Stay Positive:** Panic kills faster than the elements. Maintain a strong will to live. Focus on small, achievable tasks (building a fire, arranging a bed) to maintain a sense of control.

## Shelter Basics
Regardless of environment, your first priority in a sudden emergency is often protecting yourself from the elements.
- **Insulate:** Always put a barrier (leaves, pine needles, a backpack) between your body and the ground to prevent conductive heat loss.
- **Wind Protection:** Block prevailing winds to stop convective heat loss.
- **Size:** Make your shelter just large enough to fit your body comfortably. A shelter that is too large will rapidly lose body heat.

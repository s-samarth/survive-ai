# Communication Blackout and Internet Shutdown (India)

India orders more internet shutdowns than any other country in the world. Shutdowns happen during communal violence, farmer and student protests, exam periods, insurgency operations in Kashmir and the North East, and after major terror incidents. Separately, networks fail on their own in floods, cyclones, earthquakes, and long power cuts when tower batteries run flat.

This guide covers both: deliberate shutdowns and infrastructure failure. The survival actions are mostly the same.

---

## Part 1: Understand What Has Actually Failed

Before you assume total blackout, work out which layer is down. Your options change completely depending on the answer.

**Test in this order, it takes 90 seconds:**

1. **Do you have bars?** No bars anywhere in the building, including at the window, means the tower is down or you are being jammed. Bars but nothing works means data is blocked.
2. **Can you make a voice call?** Try a normal number. If voice works but internet does not, this is almost certainly a **deliberate data shutdown** under the Telecom Suspension Rules. Voice and SMS are usually left up.
3. **Can you send an SMS?** SMS uses the signalling channel and is the most resilient service on a mobile network. It survives congestion that kills both data and voice. It also queues — a message sent with no signal will deliver the moment a sliver of signal appears.
4. **Does 112 connect?** Emergency calls route over any available operator regardless of your SIM, and are usually exempted even during ordered shutdowns.
5. **Does a landline or broadband line work?** Fixed-line and fibre are shut down less often and less quickly than mobile data. A BSNL landline frequently survives when everything else is dark.
6. **Does FM radio work?** If yes, the transmitter and your receiver are fine and you have a one-way information channel.

**What each result means:**

| Result | Diagnosis | Your best channel |
|---|---|---|
| Voice yes, data no | Ordered data shutdown | Voice, SMS, landline, FM radio |
| Everything no, others' phones work | Your phone or SIM | Borrow a phone; try 112 without SIM |
| Nobody has anything, power is on | Tower down or full shutdown | SMS queue, landline, FM radio, physical movement |
| Nothing and no power | Infrastructure collapse | Physical movement, signalling, FM on battery radio |

## Part 2: Squeezing Communication Out of a Dying Network

**Make your battery last.**
- Airplane mode extends standby from hours to days. A phone hunting for a dead tower at full transmit power is the fastest way to drain it.
- Turn the screen to minimum brightness, switch off background sync, kill location services.
- Take the phone out of airplane mode for 5 minutes at fixed times — say, on the hour — to receive queued SMS and attempt sends. Announce this schedule to your family so everyone syncs to the same window.
- Cold and heat both destroy battery capacity. Keep the phone against your body in cold, in shade in heat.

**Get more signal.**
- Height wins. A rooftop, a terrace, an upper-floor window, or a hillside can find a tower that ground level cannot.
- Move away from concrete and metal. Reinforced concrete and metal shutters block signal badly. Step outside.
- 2G reaches much further than 4G/5G. If your phone lets you force 2G-only in network settings, do it — you lose data speed but gain kilometres of range for voice and SMS.
- Keep the phone still while sending. Movement between cells drops the attempt.

**Send messages that survive.**
- Short SMS beat long ones. Under 160 characters sends as one message; longer messages are split and can partially fail.
- Put the critical information first: **who, where, what you need, whether you are moving.**
- Good: `SAFE. Sanjay+2 kids. Sector 12 school shelter. Need insulin. Not moving.`
- Bad: a paragraph explaining what happened.
- Send the same message to three different people on three different operators. Redundancy beats eloquence.

**Use out-of-area relays.** Local networks are congested; distant ones are not. Send to a relative in another state and ask them to phone everyone else. Agree on this person in advance and make them the family's single point of contact.

## Part 3: When There Is No Network At All

**Bluetooth mesh messaging.** Apps like Bridgefy and Briar pass messages phone-to-phone over Bluetooth and local WiFi with no tower and no internet. Range is roughly 50-100 metres per hop, but messages relay through intermediate phones, so a dense crowd or a packed shelter becomes a working network. **These must be installed before the shutdown.** Install them now.

**FM radio is the most reliable information source in an Indian blackout.** All India Radio (Akashvani) maintains transmitters with backup power and broadcasts emergency information in regional languages. Many Indian phones have a physical FM chip that works with wired earphones as an antenna and needs no data. Find that app now and check it works. A separate battery or hand-crank FM radio is better because it does not drain your phone.

**Landlines and PCOs.** BSNL copper lines carry their own power from the exchange and often work during a mains failure. Hospitals, police stations, railway stations, post offices, and panchayat offices usually have working lines and, frequently, satellite phones during declared disasters.

**Physical information hubs.** During a blackout, information concentrates at fixed points. Go to:
- The nearest police station or police outpost (chowki)
- The district collectorate or tehsil office
- A government hospital or PHC
- The railway station
- A large temple, mosque, gurudwara, or church — religious institutions are usually the first functioning relief points in Indian disasters and they have loudspeakers
- The local ward office or panchayat bhavan

**Amateur radio (HAM).** India has an active ham community and licensed operators are formally integrated into disaster response through NIAR and state agencies. In a major disaster, listen around 7.05-7.10 MHz on shortwave, and ask at the collectorate whether a ham net is running.

## Part 4: Signalling When You Cannot Communicate

You may need to be found rather than to send a message.

**Sound**
- A whistle carries 3-4 times further than a shout and costs almost no energy. Three sharp blasts is the international distress signal. Repeat every minute, then listen.
- If trapped, **tap on pipes, concrete, or metal in groups of three**, then stop and listen. Rescuers use acoustic listening devices and thermal cameras; rhythmic, obviously human tapping is what they search for. Shouting fills your lungs with dust and exhausts you — tap instead.
- A car horn or a bike horn is a long-range signal. Three long blasts, pause, repeat.

**Light and visibility**
- A mirror, a steel plate, a phone screen, or a CD flashed at a helicopter or a distant road is visible for kilometres in Indian daylight.
- At night, a phone torch on strobe is visible far further than a steady beam.
- Ground signals: lay out cloth, stones, or debris in a **V** (need assistance) or **X** (need medical assistance) at least 3 metres across, in a colour that contrasts with the ground. Bright saree cloth is ideal.

**Marks for rescue teams**
- Indian search and rescue (NDRF/SDRF) mark cleared buildings with a spray-painted **X**, with quadrants noting the team, date, hazards, and number of live and dead found. If you see a completed X on a building, it has been searched.
- Mark your own location if you evacuate: write on the door in charcoal or chalk — **names, number of people, where you have gone, date and time**. This is how families find each other later.

## Part 5: Finding Each Other

**Set a family plan today, before anything happens.**
- **One out-of-state contact** everyone calls or SMSes. Local circuits jam; STD routes often do not.
- **Two physical rally points**: one just outside your building (for a fire), and one across the neighbourhood (for an evacuation). Name them precisely — "the Hanuman temple gate on MG Road", not "near the market".
- **A written card in every wallet and every school bag** with names, numbers, addresses, blood groups, and allergies. Children's phones die and children forget numbers.
- **A code word** that means "come home now, do not ask questions" and one that means "I am safe, do not look for me".

**When systems come back:**
- The **Google Person Finder** and the **IRCTC/railway enquiry** services get stood up in major Indian disasters.
- Registers are maintained at relief camps, government hospitals, and police stations. Physically visit and register your name, even if you are unhurt — this is how you get taken off missing lists.
- Post on the wall of the collectorate, the hospital, and the railway station. Paper notices work when nothing else does.

## Part 6: Staying Safe During a Shutdown-Linked Situation

Internet shutdowns in India are usually ordered because something dangerous is already happening: communal violence, a large protest, an encounter, or an insurgency operation. The shutdown is a signal, not just an inconvenience.

- **Treat a sudden shutdown as a warning to get home and stay there.** If data dies across a whole city with no storm and no power cut, assume unrest and move to safety before crowds form.
- **Section 144 CrPC / BNSS prohibitory orders** are usually imposed alongside. Gatherings of four or more can be dispersed by force. Do not stand around in groups to find out what is happening.
- **Curfew passes** are issued by the district magistrate for medical and essential needs. If someone in your house needs dialysis, chemotherapy, or insulin, get the pass early or go to the hospital before the curfew tightens.
- **Do not spread or act on unverified information.** Rumour is what turns a shutdown into a riot, and forwarding it is a criminal offence during a shutdown. In the absence of data, believe only what you see yourself, what AIR broadcasts, and what a uniformed official tells you directly.
- **Stock up before, not during.** The moment a shutdown is announced, shops empty and prices spike. Keep 7 days of essentials at home if you live in a shutdown-prone district.

## Part 7: Cash and Essentials Without UPI

A data shutdown kills UPI, card machines, and ATM networks. This has stranded entire cities.

- **Keep 5,000-10,000 rupees in small notes at home**, in 100s and 200s. Vendors during a crisis will not break a 500.
- **Petrol pumps often go cash-only** within hours. Keep the tank above half if you live somewhere shutdown-prone.
- **Ration shops (PDS)** work on Aadhaar biometric authentication, which needs connectivity. Keep some grain at home.
- Medical shops will usually still dispense against a physical prescription and cash. Carry a paper copy of any chronic prescription.

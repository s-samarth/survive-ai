# Industrial Accidents, Gas Leaks and Chemical Exposure (India)

India has dense industrial belts sitting immediately next to dense housing: Bhopal, Vapi, Ankleshwar, Manali and Ennore in Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Dahej, Taloja and Chembur in Mumbai, Ludhiana, Kanpur, Panipat, and hundreds of smaller estates. The Bhopal gas disaster of 1984 remains the world's worst industrial accident and the pattern of that night — people running into the gas rather than away from it — is the single most important lesson in this guide.

**The core rule: gas is heavier than air in almost every case that matters. Go UP and go UPWIND.**

---

## Part 1: Recognising a Release

- An unusual smell: chlorine (bleach, swimming pool), ammonia (sharp, urine-like), rotten eggs (hydrogen sulphide), garlic or almonds, sweet or fruity solvent smells, or a sharp acid bite in the throat
- **A visible cloud, mist, or haze low to the ground**, often white, yellow-green, or brown
- **A siren from a nearby factory.** Most Indian chemical plants have an on-site emergency siren. Know what yours sounds like.
- People, animals, or birds collapsing, coughing, or fleeing
- Sudden eye watering, throat burning, or coughing across many people at once
- Vegetation suddenly wilting or discolouring

**Important:** **hydrogen sulphide destroys your sense of smell within seconds.** If a rotten-egg smell disappears suddenly while you are still in the area, that is a lethal sign, not a good one. Get out.

---

## Part 2: Immediate Actions

**Decide first: escape or shelter.**

**ESCAPE if** you can see the cloud, you know where it is, and there is a clear route upwind or uphill that does not pass through it.

**SHELTER if** you cannot tell where the cloud is, it is already around you, or you are downwind with no clear route out.

### If escaping:

1. **Move UPWIND — into the wind, so the wind is in your face.** Check the wind direction with dust, smoke, a flag, or a wet finger.
2. **Move UPHILL and go UP inside a building.** Chlorine, ammonia (in cold conditions), LPG, and most industrial vapours collect at ground level and flow into low ground, drains, basements, and pits.
3. **Move at right angles to the wind, then upwind.** Running straight away downwind keeps you inside the plume.
4. **Cover your nose and mouth with a cloth, wet if possible.** For ammonia and chlorine, a cloth wet with plain water helps considerably.
5. **Do not run.** Running triples your breathing rate and pulls more gas into your lungs. **Walk fast, breathe shallowly through the cloth.** This is precisely the error that killed thousands in Bhopal.
6. **Do not go into a basement, an underground parking, or a low-lying area.**
7. **Take children with you — carry them.** They are shorter and therefore deeper in the gas layer.
8. **Do not use a vehicle if it means driving through the cloud.**

### If sheltering in place:

1. **Go to the highest floor**, to a room with the fewest windows and doors, preferably an interior bathroom.
2. **Close all doors and windows.** Switch off all AC, exhaust fans, chimney, and any ventilation that draws outside air in.
3. **Seal the room.** Wet cloth or tape along door gaps, tape over vents and keyholes, plastic sheet over windows.
4. **Keep a wet cloth over your nose and mouth.**
5. **Turn on a radio** for instructions.
6. **Stay put until the all-clear.** Do not go out to check.
7. If you must leave later, cover up completely, cover your face, and go upwind.

## Part 3: Specific Common Indian Hazards

**Chlorine** (water treatment plants, swimming pools, bleaching, transported in cylinders)
- Yellow-green gas, smells of bleach, much heavier than air, hugs the ground
- **Go up and upwind.** Never go down.
- Causes burning eyes, throat, coughing, and chest tightness; at high concentration, fluid in the lungs (pulmonary oedema) hours later
- **Anyone exposed needs 24 hours of observation** even if they feel fine

**Ammonia** (cold storages, ice plants, dairies, fertiliser units — extremely common in India)
- Sharp, unmistakable, intensely irritating
- Lighter than air when warm but forms a dense cold cloud on release, so it can hug the ground initially
- **Water is your friend** — a wet cloth over the face genuinely works because ammonia dissolves readily in water
- Flush eyes and skin with lots of water for at least 20 minutes

**Hydrogen sulphide** (sewers, septic tanks, tanneries, oil refining, drains)
- Rotten eggs at low concentration, **odourless at lethal concentration**
- This is what kills sanitation workers in Indian septic tanks and manholes every year
- **Never enter a manhole, septic tank, sewer, or unventilated pit to rescue someone who has collapsed.** Multiple deaths in a chain — the rescuer, then the next rescuer — is the standard Indian pattern. Ventilate first, and use a rope and a harness from outside.

**LPG and natural gas** (household, industrial, pipelines)
- LPG is heavier than air and pools at floor level, in drains, and in basements
- **No sparks. No switches. No phones. No flames.** Ventilate, close the valve, evacuate, call **1906**

**Pesticides and organophosphates** (agricultural India, both accidental and intentional)
- Absorbed through skin, lungs, and gut
- Signs: pinpoint pupils, excessive salivation and sweating, vomiting, muscle twitching, difficulty breathing, confusion
- **Remove contaminated clothing, wash the skin thoroughly with soap and water**, and get to a hospital urgently — atropine is the antidote and PHCs stock it
- **Do not induce vomiting.** Take the container or a photograph of the label.

**Acids and alkalis** (industrial, and acid attacks)
- **Flush with large volumes of running water for at least 20-30 minutes, continuously.**
- **Do not try to neutralise** — the reaction generates heat and worsens the burn
- Remove contaminated clothing while flushing
- Alkali burns (caustic soda, lime) are deeper and more damaging than acid burns and need longer flushing

**Radiological sources** (industrial radiography, hospital equipment, orphaned sources in scrapyards)
- If you find an unfamiliar heavy metal container with a trefoil radiation symbol, **do not touch it, do not open it, do not take it to a scrap dealer.** Move away, keep others away, and inform the police and the AERB.
- Time, distance, and shielding are the three protections. Increase all three.

## Part 4: Decontamination

For anyone who has been in a chemical cloud or splashed:

1. **Get to fresh air and away from the source first.**
2. **Remove clothing — cut it off rather than pulling it over the head.** Removing outer clothing removes the large majority of contamination.
3. **Bag the clothing** and keep it away from people. Do not shake it out.
4. **Wash with lots of water and soap**, from head down, for at least 15 minutes. Lukewarm, not hot — hot water opens pores and increases absorption.
5. **Do not scrub hard** — abrasion drives chemicals into the skin.
6. **Flush eyes for 20 minutes** with clean water, holding the eyelids open, from the inner corner outward so contaminated water does not run into the other eye. **Remove contact lenses.**
7. **For dry powder, brush it off first** before adding water. Some chemicals react violently with water.
8. **Wash hair last**, and do not use conditioner.

---

## Part 5: Treating the Exposed

- **Protect yourself first.** Contaminated casualties will contaminate you. Use gloves, cover your face, and work upwind.
- **Fresh air and rest.** Do not let them walk or exert themselves — exertion increases absorption and worsens lung injury.
- **Sit them upright** if they are breathless.
- **Give oxygen if available.**
- **Do not give anything by mouth** if they are drowsy or vomiting.
- **Watch for delayed lung injury.** Chlorine, phosgene, ammonia, and smoke can all cause fluid in the lungs 6-24 hours after exposure, in someone who initially seemed fine. **Any significant inhalation exposure needs 24 hours of observation.**
- **Keep the chemical name or a photograph of the container** and send it with them to hospital.
- **Poison information:** the **National Poisons Information Centre at AIIMS Delhi (011-26593677 / 26589391)** operates 24 hours.

---

## Part 6: If You Live Next to an Industrial Estate

- **Find out what is stored there.** Under the Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules and the Chemical Accidents (Emergency Planning, Preparedness and Response) Rules, Major Accident Hazard (MAH) units must have an off-site emergency plan and the district must publish it. Ask the district collectorate or the factory inspectorate.
- **Learn the plant siren signal** and what it means.
- **Know the prevailing wind direction** for your area in each season, and identify your upwind escape route in advance.
- **Identify the assembly point** in the off-site emergency plan.
- **Keep N95 masks and a supply of clean water at home.**
- **Participate in mock drills** if your district runs them. They are the only realistic rehearsal you will get.
- **Do not build or sleep in the low-lying area between the plant and the housing.** Gas flows downhill.

## Part 7: Confined Spaces — Septic Tanks, Manholes, Wells, Silos

This kills more Indians every year than any industrial disaster, and it is entirely preventable.

- **Never enter a septic tank, manhole, sewer, disused well, grain silo, or tanker without testing the atmosphere, forced ventilation, a harness with a rope tended from outside, and a breathing apparatus.**
- **Manual scavenging is illegal in India** under the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013. Employing anyone to enter a sewer or septic tank without protective equipment is a criminal offence.
- **If someone collapses inside: DO NOT GO IN AFTER THEM.** This is how two, three, and four people die instead of one. The gas that dropped them will drop you within seconds.
- **From outside:** shout for help, call 112 and the fire service, ventilate with a blower or by fanning, and if there is a rope on the person, pull them out from outside.
- **The atmosphere may be oxygen-deficient rather than toxic.** You cannot smell missing oxygen. Unconsciousness is instant.
